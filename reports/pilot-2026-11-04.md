# Pilot runs, 4 November 2026

The first runs of the paper-2 waiting-room mechanisms, one run per setting,
in a 4-core cloud container (not the paper machine). Treat these as checks
that the mechanisms work and as guidance for the grids, not as results.
Design A's capacity calibrated here at 195 students/s (the paper machine:
about 224). Scenario: `bench/scenarios/paper2.yaml`. Design D-A, first come
first served, token tickets.

## At 2× A's capacity

| | Paper-1 room (no lease) | Lease 1 | Lease 2 + closed loop (500 ms target) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Students | 13,128 | 13,164 | 13,154 |
| Served correctly | 91.1% | 99.5% | 100% |
| Served within 10 s | 10.3% | 10.2% | 10.5% |
| Admission wait, median / 95th pct | 29.4 s / 88.1 s | 27.6 s / 38.3 s | 49.8 s / 67.1 s |
| Backend requests per student (counted by the room) | 1.91 | 1.04 | 1.10 |
| Lease expiries (re-queued, then re-admitted) | — | 1,789 | — |
| Kendall's τ, arrival vs admission (pre-release arrivals) | 0.57 | 0.58 | 0.53 |
| Extra wait of students who pressed refresh (median) | 6.1 s | 5.2 s | 20.5 s |
| CPU-seconds per 1,000 served | 13.6 | 9.9 | 11.3 |
| Room at its CPU limit (share of seconds) | 68% | 54% | 66% |
| Admission rate, mean (min–max) | 156 fixed | 156 fixed | 93 (20–195) |

What the pilot suggests:

- **A one-request lease does what it was designed to do.** Backend requests
  per student fell from 1.91 to 1.04, the 95th-percentile wait from 88 s
  to 38 s, and nearly every student was served, at 27% less CPU per student
  served.
- **The closed loop with a 500 ms target was too cautious.** Its rate
  averaged 93/s against the fixed 156/s, so the median wait almost doubled.
  A's capacity was calibrated against a 2 s deadline, so the grids now use a
  2 s target and keep 500 ms as a sensitivity variant.
- With the closed loop, refreshing students waited 20 s longer than others;
  worth watching in `p2-adaptive`.

## At 5× A's capacity: the room itself ran out of resources

Both runs (paper-1 room, and lease 2) are unusable. The room used its whole
0.25 CPU from 5 s after release, requests piled up inside it, its memory grew
from 13 MB to its 512 MB limit and it was killed between about 60 and 100 s
after release; every later request failed. Served: 13.2% and 27.9%.

- The room's own logic is cheap: answering one queued poll costs about 7 µs
  in-process (`go test -bench QueuedPoll ./internal/wroom/`), so a quarter
  CPU could answer about 35,000 polls/s. The CPU goes into connection
  handling: under overload, students' timeouts cancel requests, which closes
  connections on both sides of the proxy, and new ones are opened constantly.
- **Measurement caveat that may affect paper 1.** The generator's
  `backend_amplification` counts a request that timed out while still
  *inside the room* as a backend request. If the room was near its CPU limit
  in paper 1's `paper-wr-long` runs, part of the reported 3.21 backend
  requests per student for token tickets may be time spent in the room, not
  at A. Check on the paper machine:

  ```bash
  python3 -c "from pathlib import Path; from analysis import collect; import sys; [print(p.name, {k: collect.load_run(p, False).get(k) for k in ('wr_cpu_saturated_share', 'wr_oom_killed', 'backend_amplification', 'wr_backend_amplification')}) for p in sorted(Path('runs/paper-wr-long').iterdir()) if p.is_dir()]"
  ```

  `wr_backend_amplification` (the room's own count of forwarded requests) is
  the right measure for waiting-room designs, and the analysis now reports it
  and flags runs where the room was at its CPU limit or was OOM-killed.
- The new grid `p2-roomcpu` compares a 0.25-CPU and a 0.5-CPU room at 5×.

- `p2-adaptive`, `p2-eta`, `p2-loads`: closed-loop target latency 2 s.
- `p2-loads`: added lease 1, and lease 1 with closed loop.
- New `p2-roomcpu` grid.
- Analysis: `wr_backend_amplification`, `wr_cpu_saturated_share`, `wr_oom_killed`.
